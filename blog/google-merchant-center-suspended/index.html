Google Merchant Center Suspended? Common Causes and How to Fix Them

By Moses Daniel, founder of Moses Mastery

On this page

  1. Read the reason Google gives you
  2. The most common causes
  3. Fix the store and the feed at the source
  4. Request a review, once
  5. Prevent it happening again
  6. When to get help
  7. Common questions

Quick answer

If your Google Merchant Center account is suspended, your products stop showing in free listings and Shopping ads until the problem is fixed and Google approves a review. The quickest way out is to read the exact reason shown in your account, fix it at the source (your store or your feed), and only then request a review. This guide covers the causes we see most often and how to work through them.

1. Read the reason Google gives you

Open your Merchant Center account and look at the notification and the Diagnostics or “Needs attention” area. Google names the issue, for example a policy violation, a missing detail on your website or a problem with your products. Every fix below depends on that message, so write it down before you change anything. Fixing a different problem than the one Google flagged is the most common reason a review fails.

2. The most common causes

  • Missing or unclear business information. Shoppers and Google need to be able to see who you are: a real contact email or phone number, a business name and an easy way to reach you.
  • Missing or vague policies. A clear return and refund policy, shipping information and terms should be easy to find in your footer and written in plain words.
  • Prices or availability that don’t match. If the price or stock status in your feed differs from the product page, Google treats it as a mismatch. Sync issues after a sale or a price change are a typical trigger.
  • Product data problems. Missing identifiers (such as GTIN or brand where they apply), poor titles, images with overlays or watermarks, or text that doesn’t describe the product.
  • Checkout and trust problems. A checkout that doesn’t work, a site that isn’t served over HTTPS, or payment pages that look unreliable.
  • Restricted or prohibited products. Some categories are limited or not allowed in Shopping. Check Google’s current policy list for your products.

3. Fix the store and the feed at the source

Update the website first, because that is what Google crawls. Add or clarify contact details, policies and shipping information. Make sure every product page shows the same price and availability as your feed. Then fix the feed: titles, descriptions, images and identifiers. If you use Shopify’s Google & YouTube channel, confirm the products are syncing and review any warnings listed there.

4. Request a review, once

When the fixes are live, request a review in Merchant Center and explain briefly what you changed. Don’t submit repeated requests while one is pending, and don’t open a new account to get around a suspension, since that can lead to further problems. Google makes the final decision and the timing varies, so no one can promise a result or a deadline.

5. Prevent it happening again

  • Check the Diagnostics tab weekly, especially after price changes, sales or theme updates.
  • Keep policies, contact details and shipping information current.
  • Use consistent business details across your site, Merchant Center and Google Business Profile.
  • Test checkout from a phone after any app or theme change.

When to get help

If you have fixed what Google named and the review still fails, or the account message is vague, a second pair of eyes helps. Our Google Merchant Center help covers account setup, feed cleanup and fixing the store issues behind policy problems. Send us your store link and the message Google gave you. Before you run Shopping ads, it also pays to work through the store audit checklist.

Common questions

Why was my Merchant Center account suspended?

Usually because of a policy problem: missing or unclear business information, vague return or shipping policies, price or availability mismatches between your feed and your site, product data issues or checkout trust problems. The exact reason is shown in your account.

How long does a review take?

It varies, and Google decides. Make sure the fixes are complete before you request it, since a rejected review can cost you time.

Can I just create a new account?

Not as a workaround. Creating new accounts to avoid a suspension can lead to further suspensions. Fix the underlying problems and request a review.

Do I need a GTIN for every product?

Not every product has one, but where a product has a manufacturer GTIN or brand, Google expects it to be provided. Check Google’s current requirements for your category.
