Shopify Product Page SEO: A Practical Checklist

Quick answer

Good Shopify product page SEO means each product page clearly states what the product is, is unique, loads fast and gives Google structured information about price and availability. Work through this checklist for your top products first, because those pages usually earn the most.

1. Page title and meta description

Shopify’s “Search engine listing” section on each product lets you edit the page title and meta description. Lead the title with what the product is, add a distinguishing detail, and keep it short enough to avoid being cut off in results (around 60 characters is a sensible target). Write the meta description as a short pitch: what it is, who it is for and one reason to click. Google may rewrite it, but a good one often helps.

2. Product name (H1) and URL

The product title becomes the main heading. Make it descriptive and natural. Keep the URL handle short and readable (for example “leather-card-holder”). If you change a handle on a live product, Shopify can create a redirect; keep that option on.

3. A unique description

Don’t paste the manufacturer’s text, since other stores use it too. Describe the product in your own words: what it is made of, sizes, uses, care and who it suits. Use short paragraphs and a few bullets so it is easy to scan. Include the phrases buyers search for where they fit, without repeating them mechanically.

4. Images and alt text

Use sharp, correctly sized images in a modern format, and add alt text that describes what is in the picture. Name files sensibly before uploading. Oversized images are a common cause of slow mobile pages, which hurts both rankings and sales (see the conversion checklist).

5. Structured data

Most Shopify themes output product structured data (name, image, price, availability) automatically. Don’t assume it’s correct: test a product URL with Google’s Rich Results Test and fix any errors, and make sure apps aren’t adding a second, conflicting version. Accurate price and availability data also helps with Google Merchant Center.

6. Variants and duplicate URLs

Shopify products often appear under several URLs, such as through collections. Themes normally set a canonical tag pointing to the main product URL, which is what you want. Check that yours does, and avoid creating separate near-identical products for every colour unless there is a real reason.

7. Reviews and trust

Genuine reviews help shoppers and give the page fresh, relevant text. Show shipping, returns and payment information near the buy button so buyers don’t leave to look for it. Only show reviews from real customers.

8. Internal links

Link to the product from its collection, related products and relevant blog posts, and link out from the product to a size guide or care page where helpful. Use descriptive link text.

9. Out-of-stock and discontinued products

Don’t simply delete a page that has traffic or backlinks. If the product is coming back, keep the page and say so. If it is gone for good, redirect it to the closest replacement or its collection.

10. Measure what happens

In Google Search Console, check which product pages get impressions and clicks, and what queries they appear for. Improve the pages with many impressions but few clicks first.

Common questions

How long should a Shopify page title be?

Around 60 characters is a sensible target so it isn’t cut off in results. Put the most important words first.

Do I need to write unique product descriptions?

Yes. Copying manufacturer text means your page says the same as every other seller, which gives Google little reason to rank yours.

Does Shopify add product schema automatically?

Most themes do, but check with Google’s Rich Results Test, because apps and theme edits can break or duplicate it.

What should I do with discontinued products?

Redirect them to a close alternative or the collection page, rather than leaving a dead page or deleting one that has traffic.
